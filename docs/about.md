## Documentation Philosophy

Good documentation should be accurate, findable, maintainable, and respectful of the reader's time. I do not see documentation as a final delivery artifact that appears after the product is done. I see it as a product surface that helps users understand what exists, what matters, what to do next, and how to recover when something goes wrong.

My documentation philosophy is built around four principles:

<div class="principle-grid" markdown>

- :material-account-search-outline:

    **Start with the audience**

    Documentation should match the reader's role, context, vocabulary, and technical depth.

- :material-map-search-outline:

    **Design the information architecture**

    Strong navigation, consistent labels, and useful categories make content easier to scan and maintain.

- :material-format-list-checks:

    **Write for action**

    Procedures, examples, prerequisites, expected results, and troubleshooting paths help readers finish real tasks.

- :material-source-branch:

    **Keep content maintainable**

    Markdown, Git, MkDocs, Sphinx, reusable patterns, and review workflows help documentation stay current.

</div>

## Writing Process

My writing process balances product understanding, technical validation, and reader experience.

1. **Understand the audience and goal**  
   Identify who the content is for, what they need to accomplish, and what level of detail they expect.

2. **Collect source information**  
   Review product behavior, SME inputs, API details, UI flows, release notes, tickets, and available engineering references.

3. **Design the content structure**  
   Define the page type, heading structure, information flow, navigation placement, and related content paths.

4. **Write and test the draft**  
   Create clear Markdown content with prerequisites, steps, examples, notes, warnings, and validation points.

5. **Review and refine**  
   Incorporate SME feedback, check terminology, remove ambiguity, improve scanability, and prepare the content for publication.

6. **Maintain and improve**  
   Update content through product changes, support insights, search behavior, release cycles, and documentation audits.
